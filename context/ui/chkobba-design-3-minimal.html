Direction 3 · Modern Minimal

Dark, flat, high contrast and built for one thumb: oversized card indices, a fanned hand in the thumb zone, capture choices in a bottom sheet, a timer ring around whoever is on turn, and compact score chips with visible pile counts. In frame b, tap an option row to preview, tap again to confirm; use the hand chips to switch card; close the sheet with the x to see the fan and tap a card to reopen it.

UX improvements proposed

  • Capture bottom sheet in the thumb zone. Options are 56px rows with numbered colour tags; the same tag appears on the table cards. The selected card and your other cards sit in the sheet header as chips, so you can switch card without closing the sheet. Proposal for the real build: press-and-hold previews an option, release confirms (one-handed, no second tap).
  • Timer ring on the active avatar. A draining arc in the team colour shows whose turn it is and how long they have; inactive avatars drop to a faint team-coloured outline and muted name. It doubles as the "bot is thinking" cue and a future turn-timeout.
  • Last-played marker. The newest table card is labelled with the player who put it down (here "KARIM" in coral), and on a capture the played card stays visible, outlined in lime, as it flies to the pile with the captured cards.
  • Pile counts always visible. Each score chip shows cards captured, sweeps and a thin progress line to 21, which also makes the Karta and Dineri races readable mid-round.
  • Minimal CHKOBBA. A single slam of the word, lime rays, a flash outline on the table and a "+3 cards" floating off the sweeper's pile, with no confetti. Fast enough not to interrupt a rapid game.

React Native notes

  • Everything is Views + Text + react-native-svg (suits, timer ring via Circle strokeDasharray/strokeDashoffset animated with Animated/Reanimated).
  • Fan = absolutely-positioned cards with transform: [{translateY},{rotate}] and transformOrigin (RN 0.73+) or a wrapper View for the pivot.
  • Bottom sheet: @gorhom/bottom-sheet or a plain animated View; no blur used.
  • Fonts: Inter, Space Grotesk (numerals), Cairo (Arabic labels).

Risks

  • Least ownable of the three: flat dark card games are common, so it relies on polish and motion for identity and nothing says "Tunisia".
  • Oversized indices mean no pips, so players cannot visually count suit symbols (Dineri). The pile chips help but this removes a classic cue.
  • The sheet hides the fan while open, so you cannot see the chosen card in context; the header card and chips only partly compensate.
  • Table area is the tightest of the three: 10 table cards (3 rows) need the avatars and log to stay compact.
