Chkobba Online · three visual directions

Three clearly different looks for the same screens: home, your turn with a capture choice, a bot's turn with a CHKOBBA sweep, and the round summary (plus game-over). All three share the same sample game state and the same capture logic, so they can be compared like for like. Each prototype is interactive: tap hand cards, preview and confirm captures, switch the summary between round-done and game-over.

1

Café Tunisien

warm · traditional · bilingualOpen prototype →

Rationale

A game at a Tunis café: wooden table rim, green baize, cream cards with real pips and illustrated face cards (Ray crowned, Moujira with a jasmine band, Kawel in a chechia), brass nameplates, and a paper racham with a hand-drawn tally for the score. Arabic terms (شكبّة, كارطة, ديناري, برميلة, سبعة الحيّة) sit next to the Latin ones, so the game's own vocabulary becomes part of the look. The CHKOBBA moment is a rubber stamp with confetti.

Strengths

  • Strongest cultural authenticity and warmth
  • Classic pips make suit-counting (Dineri) natural
  • Racham tally is a memorable score display

Risks

  • Highest art and polish cost
  • Small pips at 46px table size; relies on large indices
  • Can read dated or "skin-like" to younger players
2

Sidi Bou Said

bright · brand identity · friendlyOpen prototype →

Rationale

Tunisian blue and white with a zellige star pattern, an arched table shaped like the village's blue doors, and round avatars with team-coloured rings (blue for us, orange for them). Cards are rounded and modern with chunky indices and emblem face cards (crown, jasmine, chechia). Buttons have a solid "press" depth that is cheap to build in React Native. The result is light, friendly and instantly recognisable in an app-store grid, and it gives the brand a mascot-able world (the door, the cat on the wall, jasmine).

Strengths

  • Most distinctive and ownable; great store presence
  • Team colours make 2v2 readable at a glance
  • Strong turn callout and colour-coded capture options

Risks

  • Light UI is harsh in dark rooms and on OLED battery
  • Arched board costs vertical space for 10 table cards
  • Motifs need respectful, commissioned illustration
3

Modern Minimal

dark · flat · one-handedOpen prototype →

Rationale

Optimised for the way the game is actually played: one thumb, often in short sessions. Everything that needs a tap lives in the bottom third: a fanned hand and a capture bottom sheet with 56px option rows. Oversized card indices stay readable at any size, a draining timer ring marks whose turn it is, and compact score chips keep captured-pile counts and sweep counts visible all the time. The CHKOBBA celebration is a single fast slam rather than a full-screen animation.

Strengths

  • Best usability, legibility and contrast
  • Cheapest to build and easiest to theme or localise
  • Timer ring doubles as a future turn-timeout UI

Risks

  • Least ownable; nothing says Tunisia
  • No pips, so no visual suit counting
  • Sheet hides the fan while choosing a capture

New direction (October 2026)

One more look built on a shared kit (chkobba-proto-kit.js), so it uses the same sample game and capture logic as the ones above. It has a deck menu and an in-app deck picker on its home screen, and works with all 13 decks in the deck gallery (five of them new).

6

Sahara Night

dark · calm · atmosphericOpen prototype →

Rationale

A game on a woven mat in the desert after sunset. Seats are lantern medallions whose gold ring burns down as the turn clock. The score is a caravan walking a 21-step track to an oasis, so you can see at a glance who is closer to winning. Capture options are numbered stars. A CHKOBBA is a shooting star, and then شكبّة is written out in gold strokes.

Strengths

  • Most beautiful dark theme; suits OLED and night play
  • The caravan track shows how close each team is to 21, not just the score
  • Calm pacing fits long casual sessions

Risks

  • Round mat holds fewer table cards than a rectangle
  • Less energy than Neon Souk for competitive players

Suggested path

These are not mutually exclusive at the interaction level. My suggestion is to take Direction 2 as the brand and visual identity (it is the one a stranger would remember), and borrow two interaction ideas from Direction 3: the capture bottom sheet in the thumb zone (or at least 56px option rows above the hand) and the timer ring on the active seat. From Direction 1, keep the Arabic vocabulary in the summary and the racham-style tally as an optional score view. Add a dark theme to Direction 2 early, since the light palette is its biggest practical risk. The numbered capture badges (option 1/2 linked to table cards) work in all three and are the single most useful UX change over the current build.
